*How the promises in Channels are proven.*

# Channels — proof

Proves [specs/product/channels.md](../../specs/product/channels.md).

## Coverage model

The channel-lifecycle spine continues to use the channel sidebar in a real browser, as the testing directive requires. Product assertions now stay at behavior altitude: creation proves that the resulting channel is empty and unpinned, the general newest-first assertion below proves its ordering consequence, and rename-at-birth remains with the owning UI proofs. Focus acceptance covers both selection surfaces without moving their interaction details here. The existing sidebar walk proves one two-client focus path; one additional row starts the selecting client collapsed and proves that a switcher selection reaches the other client. [Navbar acceptance](../ui/app/top-bar/index.md#^top-ac-switcher-select) owns opening, local selection and dismissal, keyboard behavior, and the open-channel no-op. [Switcher drag acceptance](../ui/app/top-bar/index.md#^top-ac-switcher-drag) owns the popover drag boundary; the existing two-client pinning walk continues to prove shared pin state.

## Assertions

### Acceptance criteria

Shared declaration ([arch/testing-policy.md#^declaration-schema](../../specs/arch/testing-policy.md#^declaration-schema)): every terminal assertion below is **acceptance** shape — the outer boundary is a real browser against a really-running server, with two connected browsers where another client's outcome is part of the claim. Channels, artifacts, initial focus, and initial pins are fixtures created through the production API or CLI; deletion-cascade artifacts point to a temporary file and a local request-recording HTTP target so unchanged bytes and absence of mutating requests are observable. No test hook replaces a production mechanism. Real-motion declaration ([arch/testing-policy.md#^motion-rule](../../specs/arch/testing-policy.md#^motion-rule)): no assertion below claims motion, so each may use the default motion override — a mock that forfeits coverage of the motion itself.

The shell-wide disconnection promise (^ch-disconnect) is not re-proven here: its coverage is the app shell's connection-state assertions ([ui/app/index.md](../../specs/ui/app/index.md), Connection states). The server event-ordering part of deletion is carried by [arch/channel-state/index.md#^cs-ac-pin-prune](../arch/channel-state/index.md#^cs-ac-pin-prune), which crosses a real HTTP/websocket deletion and observes artifact removals before channel removal and the resulting pin/focus events; the product deletion paths below do not order a second event-stream permutation.

- Under [the channel-creation promise](../../specs/product/channels.md#^ch-create), creating through the channel sidebar yields a server channel with no artifacts or tab pages and with no pin — *(covered by test: `packages/web/test/e2e/channel-product.test.ts` “channel lifecycle product outcomes > creation, rename, cancellation, and confirmed deletion converge without touching targets”)*. ^ch-ac-create
- Creating a channel in one client makes it appear in another connected client's channel sidebar, in the correct group and position; deleting it removes it from that client — *(covered by test: `packages/web/test/e2e/channel-product.test.ts` “channel lifecycle product outcomes > creation, rename, cancellation, and confirmed deletion converge without touching targets”)*. ^ch-ac-lifecycle-sync
- Renaming a channel in one client shows the new name in another connected client, with the same channel identity, position, tab pages, and artifact contents — *(covered by test: `packages/web/test/e2e/channel-product.test.ts` “channel lifecycle product outcomes > creation, rename, cancellation, and confirmed deletion converge without touching targets”)*. ^ch-ac-rename
- Confirming Delete from the channel sidebar removes the channel from server storage and from the connected channel sidebar — *(covered by test: `packages/web/test/e2e/channel-crud.test.ts` “deleting a channel through the permanent sidebar removes it from server storage”)*. ^ch-ac-delete
- Cancelling deletion leaves the channel, every referenced artifact record, and every target unchanged; confirming it removes the channel metadata and all referenced artifact records while leaving their filesystem paths and remote URLs untouched — *(covered by test: `packages/web/test/e2e/channel-product.test.ts` “channel lifecycle product outcomes > creation, rename, cancellation, and confirmed deletion converge without touching targets”)*. ^ch-ac-delete-cascade
- Deleting the focused channel lands on the first pinned channel; with no pinned channels, the newest unpinned channel; deleting the last channel lands on the no-channels empty state — *(covered by test: `packages/web/test/e2e/channel-product.test.ts` “channel lifecycle product outcomes > focused deletion selects pinned, newest recent, and no-channel successors”)*. ^ch-ac-delete-selection
- Pinning, unpinning, and reordering pinned channels in one client is reflected in another connected client — *(covered by test: `packages/web/test/e2e/channel-product.test.ts` “channel lifecycle product outcomes > pin append, marked drag placement, unpin, and reorder converge in both clients”)*. ^ch-ac-pin-sync
- Pinning via the simple mechanism places the channel at the end of the pinned list; pinning by drag places it at the dropped position; unpinning returns it to its creation-ordered slot in the unpinned group — *(covered by test: `packages/web/test/e2e/channel-product.test.ts` “channel lifecycle product outcomes > pin append, marked drag placement, unpin, and reorder converge in both clients”)*. ^ch-ac-pin-placement
- Unpinned channels list newest first, and focusing a channel does not change its position — *(covered by test: `packages/web/test/e2e/channel-product.test.ts` “channel lifecycle product outcomes > shared focus follows both clients without reordering newest-first recent channels”)*. ^ch-ac-order
- Focusing a channel from the channel sidebar in one client changes the channel every connected client shows — *(covered by test: `packages/web/test/e2e/channel-product.test.ts` “channel lifecycle product outcomes > shared focus follows both clients without reordering newest-first recent channels”)*. ^ch-ac-focus-broadcast
- Under [the shared-focus promise](../../specs/product/channels.md#^ch-focus-broadcast), with one client started collapsed from an authored browser-storage fixture, selecting a different channel from the navbar switcher changes the channel that client and a second connected client show, without reload in either client; the selecting client remains collapsed. The storage fixture is authored data consumed by the production preference reader, not a replaced mechanism — *(covered by test: `packages/web/test/e2e/channel-product.test.ts` “switcher focus reaches both clients while the selecting client stays collapsed”)*. ^ch-ac-switcher-focus-broadcast
