## Assertions

### Acceptance criteria

Shared browser declaration ([arch/testing-policy.md#^declaration-schema](../../specs/arch/testing-policy.md#^declaration-schema)): the browser assertions below are **acceptance** shape — the outer boundary is a real browser against a really-running server, with two connected browsers where shared channel focus is part of the claim. Channels, pages, and artifacts are fixtures created through the production API or CLI; no test hook replaces a production mechanism. Real-motion declaration ([arch/testing-policy.md#^motion-rule](../../specs/arch/testing-policy.md#^motion-rule)): no assertion below claims motion. The horizontal, vertical, focused-tab, and remaining focus-continuity walks use the default motion override, a mock that forfeits coverage of motion itself; the dead-zone matrix leaves authored CSS motion enabled but makes no transition comparison.

Electron declaration: the Electron focus-continuity assertions are **acceptance** shape — the outer boundary is the real Electron application against a running server, with production webviews and native pointer and keyboard input. Authored artifact documents are fixtures. The desktop fixture-launch hook selects the application URL but replaces no navigation, focus, native-input, webview, renderer, state, or server mechanism. The assertions claim no CSS motion and use no motion override.

The chord-inside-a-document doors are proven by the bridge's browser and Electron assertions ([arch/artifact-frame/artifact-bridge.md](../../specs/arch/artifact-frame/artifact-bridge.md), Keyboard forwarding); the shell-chrome door is [arch/ui/keyboard-navigation.md#^kbn-ac-shell-door](../arch/ui/keyboard-navigation.md#^kbn-ac-shell-door)'s. Once a door delivers a chord, the page-selection and channel-focus paths converge with pointer input at the two one-operation seams ([arch/ui/keyboard-navigation.md#^kbn-ac-one-operation](../arch/ui/keyboard-navigation.md#^kbn-ac-one-operation), [arch/ui/keyboard-navigation.md#^kbn-ac-one-operation-focus](../arch/ui/keyboard-navigation.md#^kbn-ac-one-operation-focus)). Those architecture boundary tests are the complete proof of identical pointer/keyboard transitions; this product spec orders no rendered-parity comparison.

**Coverage relationship — platform breadth.** The platform-to-chord mapping — option on macOS, ctrl on Windows and Linux, any additional modifier leaving native behavior, repeat remaining a match, and composition never navigating — is platform breadth owned by [arch/ui/keyboard-navigation.md](../../specs/arch/ui/keyboard-navigation.md) for the shell listener. Its mapping row must carry a **contract** over authored platform identities and key events; the bridge retains its own browser and Electron matrices. ^tp-ac-chord-modifiers

**Coverage relationship — platform seam.** Real platform detection feeding that shell mapping is the same architecture owner's **seam**, crossed once in a running client; the acceptance assertions below exercise only the CI host's real chord and imply no multi-OS browser fleet ([arch/testing-policy.md#^platform-breadth](../../specs/arch/testing-policy.md#^platform-breadth)). ^tp-ac-chord-platform-seam

- The navigation chord steps page selection while an app-chrome text field has focus, leaves that field's caret and selection values unchanged, blurs DOM focus when it moves, and stops at the first and last page — *(covered by test: `packages/web/test/e2e/keyboard-navigation-product.test.ts` “keyboard product walks > moves between page ends from a focused chrome field without changing its text selection (^tp-ac-chord)”)*. ^tp-ac-chord
- Vertical chord traversal stops at both ends and visits channels in channel-sidebar order — pinned channels in their pin order, then unpinned newest first — and arriving on another channel updates the shared channel focus, observed in a second client — *(covered by test: `packages/web/test/e2e/keyboard-navigation-product.test.ts` “keyboard product walks > walks pinned then newest unpinned channels and publishes each focus to a second client (^tp-ac-chord-vertical)”)*. ^tp-ac-chord-vertical
- The chord pressed while a production artifact that cannot run the bridge — a raw image or PDF, or a CSP-blocked page — holds focus changes neither page selection nor channel focus; then, with focus moved to app chrome, the same chord navigates, so the negative is not vacuous. Browser only; an incompatible producer has the same accepted dead zone under the bridge's cross-version non-goal and requires no separate old-installation test fleet — *(covered by test: `packages/web/test/e2e/keyboard-navigation-product.test.ts` “keyboard product walks > keeps raw image, PDF, and CSP-blocked documents as browser dead zones until chrome focus returns (^tp-ac-chord-deadzone)”)*. ^tp-ac-chord-deadzone
- In a real browser with at least three pages, a real click on the first tab leaves it focused. Consecutive Right navigation chords select the second and third pages, the first move releases DOM focus, and the second move works without another focus action; consecutive Left chords return to the first page under the same conditions. Every move is observed through the selected tab, page, and artifact document — *(covered by test: `packages/web/test/e2e/keyboard-navigation-product.test.ts` “keyboard product walks > continues repeated page traversal across artifact, focused tab, shell background, and sidebar focus (^tp-ac-chord-focus-continuity-browser, ^tp-ac-chord-focused-tab-browser)”)*. ^tp-ac-chord-focused-tab-browser
- With at least three production artifact documents in one channel, a real click inside the first document followed by consecutive Right presses reaches the second and third documents, and consecutive Left presses return through the second to the first, with no programmatic focus reset between presses. The same uninterrupted walk repeats after a real click on non-action shell ground and after a real click in the channel-sidebar region. Every press observes the selected tab page, reached document, and frame readiness through production UI signals — *(covered by test: `packages/web/test/e2e/keyboard-navigation-product.test.ts` “keyboard product walks > continues repeated page traversal across artifact, focused tab, shell background, and sidebar focus (^tp-ac-chord-focus-continuity-browser, ^tp-ac-chord-focused-tab-browser)”)*. ^tp-ac-chord-focus-continuity-browser
- In the real Electron application with at least three pages, a native click on the first tab leaves it focused. Consecutive Right navigation chords select the second and third pages, the first move releases DOM focus, and the second move works without another focus action; consecutive Left chords return to the first page under the same conditions. Every move is observed through the selected tab, page, and production webview document — *(covered by test: `packages/desktop/test/e2e/native-navigation-key.test.ts` “continues repeated page traversal across artifact, focused tab, shell background, and sidebar focus (^tp-ac-chord-focus-continuity-electron, ^tp-ac-chord-focused-tab-electron)”)*. ^tp-ac-chord-focused-tab-electron
- The real Electron application performs the artifact-document, shell-ground, and channel-sidebar focus-origin walks over production webviews with native keyboard input. A real click inside the first webview is followed by consecutive Right and Left presses without focusing a webview or shell control between them; real shell-ground and channel-sidebar clicks begin the next two walks in that same application launch. Every press observes the selected tab page, reached webview document, and frame readiness through production UI signals — *(covered by test: `packages/desktop/test/e2e/native-navigation-key.test.ts` “continues repeated page traversal across artifact, focused tab, shell background, and sidebar focus (^tp-ac-chord-focus-continuity-electron, ^tp-ac-chord-focused-tab-electron)”)*. ^tp-ac-chord-focus-continuity-electron
